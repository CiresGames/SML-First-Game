# Red wind scarf

The Rider prefab and MantaFlight scene include `RiderScarf`, a red gathered neck
wrap and a 1.15 m × 18 cm cloth ribbon. The meshes and two-sided URP materials are
generated by **Manta / Rider / Install red wind scarf**; running it again updates
the existing assets without adding another scarf.

The floating ribbon now has two surfaces with closed edge faces, 12 mm apart.
Its thickness follows the simulated fabric normals. Body collision volumes include
half the thickness plus 15 mm of extra clearance; collision is solved after the
stretch tether to avoid pushing the cloth back into the torso. `thickness` and
`bodyClearance` are exposed on `RiderScarf`.

The ribbon uses a Verlet cloth simulation with a 90 Hz fixed step,
structural, shear and bending constraints, a pinned neck seam, gravity, air drag,
and small travelling cross-flow disturbances. Coarse head and torso collision
volumes prevent the cloth from passing through the main body. Arm, wing and
environment collisions are not included.

The simulation has 8 lengthwise sections and one strip across its width (18
particles), replacing the earlier 24 × 3 grid (100 particles). This removes
independent widthwise wrinkles and favors broad bends. Wind changes are filtered
over 0.65 s. One slow wave runs at 0.7–1.4 rad/s with force gain 1.5; damping relative
to Rider motion is 10/s. Firmer bending constraints keep the scarf steady while
allowing it to settle into a new wind direction.

The visible ribbon samples a smooth cubic curve over the cage into 32 sections;
these extra vertices add no simulated joints or independent wrinkles. Shape changes
are filtered over 0.18 s relative to the moving neck, so translation stays attached
even in fast flight. Rendered samples retain head/torso clearance and the 12 mm shell.
Teleport resets both simulation and visual smoothing history.

Flight movement is filtered separately from environmental gusts: rider velocity
and neck motion respond over 0.14 s, while wind retains its 0.65 s smoothing.
Neck motion captures the animated banking, stunt rotation and glide pose; its
contribution is capped at 12 m/s to soften mount/deployment transitions. Changes
in travel velocity add a damped inertial pull (acceleration capped at 35 m/s²,
gain 0.65), so turns, acceleration and braking affect the ribbon without adding
high-frequency flutter. Reset clears this motion history as well.

`WindManager.GetWindAt` supplies the actual local wind, including zone transitions
and gusts. Particle drag accounts for motion through the air; flutter responds to
wind relative to Rider velocity. Therefore the scarf indicates **apparent wind**:
at rest it shows the world wind; in flight it combines world wind with the Rider's
own movement. This also works while mounted on the manta.

Inspector controls: length, width, wind response, flutter and teleport reset
distance. Dimensions are read when the simulation initializes. Pausing the game
freezes the simulation; respawn/teleport resets its history to avoid stretched
fabric. Runtime meshes belong to each Rider and are released on destruction.

Validation: launch a separate batch Unity process with
`-executeMethod MantaFlight.Rider.Editor.RiderScarfValidation.RunBatch`, without
`-quit`. It checks calm air, crosswind strength/reversal, 24/144 FPS consistency,
motion through still air, teleport recovery, pause, wind-service integration and
neck attachment. Report: `Logs/MantaFlight/validation-scarf.txt`.

Root translation carries the particle cage before each frame's simulation; drag
uses apparent wind in that translating frame. This avoids pinning impulses and
bunching when fast flight spans several physics steps per rendered frame.
`RiderScarfThicknessValidation.Run` checks the closed shell, wind reversal,
steady motion, rendered flight at 24/60/144 FPS, and teleport recovery in edit mode.
It also exercises full rolls, loops and a banked descending glide trajectory.
